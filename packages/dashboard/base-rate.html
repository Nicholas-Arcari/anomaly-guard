Why rare attacks are hard

When attacks are a small share of the traffic, even an accurate detector raises mostly false alarms. Bayes' theorem gives the share of alarms that are real attacks, the positive predictive value (PPV):

PPV = TPR · prevalence / (TPR · prevalence + FPR · (1 − prevalence))

Worked example: a detector that catches 99% of attacks (TPR = 0.99) and flags 1% of legitimate requests (FPR = 0.01), on traffic where 1 request in 1,000 is an attack (prevalence = 0.001), gives PPV ≈ 0.09. Only about 9% of its alarms are real attacks: roughly ten false alarms for every true one.
