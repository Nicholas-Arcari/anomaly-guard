About The Anomaly Guard

What it is

An educational intrusion detection system, built as a group project for a master's statistics course. It watches simulated web requests and decides, for each one, whether it is normal or suspicious — in two ways. A rule engine matches known attack signatures. A statistical model learns what normal traffic looks like and flags what departs from it, with no knowledge of the attacks.

Research question: how much can statistics catch without predefined signatures, and how often does it get it wrong?

How it works

  1. Generate. A seeded simulator produces legitimate requests and attacks: canonical, obfuscated and novel. The same seed always gives the same data.
  2. Learn normal. The model is fitted on legitimate requests only, one set of statistics per endpoint and parameter.
  3. Detect. Each request gets a rule verdict and an anomaly score s = −log₁₀ p; the threshold turns the score into an alert.
  4. Evaluate. On a held-out test split: attacks caught and missed, false alarms, error rates — and why rare attacks make false alarms dominate.

The statistics, one theme per team member

  • T1 · Character distributions and chi-square — is the mix of characters in a value normal?
  • T2 · Lengths and outliers — is a value too long, or unusual in combination?
  • T3 · Evaluation and uncertainty — how often is the detector wrong, and how sure are we?
  • T4 · Probability and simulation — what does traffic look like, and what happens when attacks are rare?

Safety

Everything runs in your browser. The traffic is synthetic; the attack strings are public textbook examples, measured and shown as text, never executed or sent anywhere.
